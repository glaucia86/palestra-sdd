Technical: context is a multidimensional resource: attention, tokens, latency, quota, and review effort compete throughout the session.

Executive impact: excess context surfaces as rework, delay, and decisions that are harder to audit, not merely as a larger bill.

Control and KPI: bound the task and track first-pass acceptance, retries, review time, and total cost per accepted outcome.

Caveat: no single dimension proves quality; calibrate the budget to the risk and nature of the work.

04A / Capacity ≠ Utility

What Fits Is Not What Helps

Maximum window
Active context
Useful contextrelevant · current · actionable
maximum window ≠ active context ≠ useful context

Sources: Lost in the Middle · Chroma Context Rot

Technical: the window is nominal capacity; active context is the content present; useful context is the relevant, current, and actionable subset.

Executive impact: more capacity can expand the options, but it does not remove noise, contradictions, or review cost.

Control and KPI: measure retrieval precision, regressions, retries, and time to a verifiable decision.

Caveat: Lost in the Middle and Context Rot show conditional degradation; they do not establish a universal percentage for coding agents.

04A / Smart Zone

Watch Signals, Not a Magic Number

Smart Zoneone slice · one source of truthsingle outcome, concise evidence, clear decision
Warning zonesignals accumulatingslow review, imprecise retrieval, growing diff
Degradation zoneoperational clarity droppedcompeting goals, stale docs, retries, and verbose outputs
Tunable heuristic: end, compact, or isolate when the signals reduce confidence and verifiability.

Technical: Smart Zone names a healthy operating range; the warning zone appears before degradation and relies on observable signals.

Executive impact: detecting lost clarity early reduces long sessions, broad diffs, and unproductive review cycles.

Control and KPI: monitor simultaneous goals, retries, output volume, diff size, regressions, and review time.

Caveat: this is a tunable heuristic, not a scientific benchmark or a fixed limit for any model or context window.

04A / Cache ≠ Free Space

Cache Saves Computation; It Does Not Free the Window

What does cache do?reuses an identical prefix and may reduce cost and latency
What does it not do?cache-read tokens still count toward the context window
How do we free space?defer, edit, summarize, or isolate content that need not remain
Cache answers “what does reprocessing cost?” Context management answers “what still needs to be in the window?”

Sources: Prompt Caching · Context Windows

Technical: prompt caching avoids reprocessing an identical prefix, but cache-read tokens still compose the window; Progressive Disclosure, context editing, compaction, and isolation act on present content.

Executive impact: cache may reduce cost and latency without improving relevance or fixing stale documentation.

Control and KPI: track cache hit rate alongside window use, first-pass acceptance, regressions, and review time.

Caveat: a cache hit proves computational reuse, not the quality or relevance of reused content.

Technical: the context budget combines limits for context, cost, time, attempts, parallelism, and diff size per slice.

Executive impact: backpressure makes effort, review, and the point for stopping a degraded execution more predictable.

Control and KPI: record consumed budget, retries, WIP, diff churn, and the share of outcomes accepted on the first pass.

Caveat: the budget is a guardrail, not a consumption target; reserve capacity to verify, document failures, and produce a handoff.

Continuity

Useful handoff = state + evidence + failures + decisions + risk + next action + owner + terminal state.

Technical: the handoff transfers the minimum sufficient state, artifacts, evidence, failures, decisions, risks, next action, owner, and terminal state.

Executive impact: a new session starts at the real work boundary, reducing rediscovery and repeated decisions.

Control and KPI: measure restart time, reopened decisions, repeated checks, and handoffs that reach the next owner without extra clarification.

Caveat: a full transcript is not a substitute for synthesis; secrets, cookies, and personal data never belong in the envelope.

04A / Routing & Outcome

Route by Risk. Measure by Accepted Outcome

specifystrong reasoningambiguity and architecture
implementproportional capabilitybounded, verifiable slice
verifydeterministic toolobjective rule first
reviewindependent checkerintent, risk, and caveat
escalateperson or strong modelhigh risk or low verifiability
Total costmodel + tools + retries + latency + review÷ accepted outcomes

Technical: routing combines phase, risk, and verifiability: strong reasoning for ambiguity, deterministic tools for objective rules, and independent review for intent.

Executive impact: expensive capability is reserved for the point where it reduces risk; bounded tasks use the sufficient option, not the cheapest or most expensive by dogma.

Control and KPI: compare total cost per accepted outcome, first-pass acceptance, retries, regressions, latency, and review effort.

Caveat: aliases, availability, and rate cards change; no model family or price is a universal policy of this slide.
